# Signal-driven tail hedging: can you time the put?

> **Superseded numbers.** Produced before the engine accounting fixes and the
> chain cleaning, on a single entry calendar. See
> [`ROBUSTNESS_REVIEW.md`](ROBUSTNESS_REVIEW.md) for current results and the
> status of each claim below.

Generated by [`signal_experiments.py`](../experiments/signal_experiments.py). Question: does
gating put purchases on a regime signal (valuation, volatility, credit, trend)
beat simply always holding the cheap deep-OTM hedge? Engine config throughout:
strike-based 25–30% OTM, DTE 90–180 / exit 30, bi-monthly, daily exits,
monetize-and-reinvest, 3.3%/yr external budget, runtime invariants armed.

Data: purchased DeltaNeutral ALLSPX (SPX 1996–2025), local-only. SPX is a
price index (no dividends), so excess vs buy & hold is the like-for-like number.

## Bottom line

**No timing signal beat always-on on a risk-adjusted basis** — in either the
full 1996–2025 history or the 2007–2025 macro-signal window. This independently
reproduces Spitznagel's own argument: you can't time tail events, so the
cost-effective move is to *hold* the cheap hedge continuously, not time it.

| Window | always-on excess | Best timing signal | Best signal Sharpe vs always-on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1996–2025 (price signals) | +0.95pp, Sharpe **0.430** | drawdown>10% +1.51pp but DD −56.5, Sharpe 0.287 | none beat 0.430 |
| 2007–2025 (macro signals) | +3.01pp, Sharpe **0.486** | VIX<15 +2.13pp but DD −58.4, Sharpe 0.312 | none beat 0.486 |

## Why timing fails (the decomposition is the point)

Several signals *do* fire at the right times — `realized-vol LOW`,
`within 3% of high`, `VIX<15`, `HY-spread tight` each hedged 3–6 times in the
12 months *before* the GFC/COVID/2022 crashes. But they still lose, because:

1. **Crashes are rare; false alarms dominate.** The premium bled across all the
   calm periods that also tripped the signal swamps the crash payoff.
2. **Part-time hedging leaves you unprotected when it counts.** Being hedged
   only ~30–50% of the time means you're often naked when a crash *starts* →
   *worse* max-drawdown and Sharpe than always-on, even when total return edges up.
3. **The only positive-excess signals are reactive** (`below 200d MA`,
   `drawdown>10%`): they hedge *after* the fall begins (decompose: 0 before /
   N during), catching the back half of long grinds (dot-com, GFC) — but they
   worsen drawdown and have poor Sharpe.

## Two cautionary tales (kept as worked examples)

- **The +12pp "Tobin's Q" false discovery (round 1, now removed).** An
  expanding-median threshold on a *mislabeled* `tobin_q` column (a copy of a
  strictly-falling series — see `scripts/fetch_signals.py`) resolved to "the
  year is 2007–2009," i.e. hedge *during* the one big in-sample crash. The
  decomposition exposed it: 0 hedge-dates before any crash, all 19 inside
  2007–2009, nothing in COVID/2022. Lesson: **fixed absolute thresholds, never
  expanding-median; and always decompose.**
- **`Buffett > 180` best max-DD (−26%).** Looks like a DD win, but it only ever
  fired in 2021–22 (N=22), caught 2022, and missed GFC/COVID entirely. A
  tiny-sample coincidence, not an edge — flagged by the decomposition.

## Caveats / what would change the verdict

- **always-on is itself window-dependent**: +0.95pp / Sharpe 0.430 over the full
  history (≈ neutral) vs +3pp / 0.486 from a 2007 start that front-loads the
  GFC. Not a free lunch either.
- **Macro signals are weak here**: 2007-only coverage, revision lookahead risk,
  and `tobin_q` is a broken proxy. A genuine point-in-time **Shiller CAPE** is
  the missing input to test the valuation-timing thesis honestly — the current
  data cannot.
- **Monetization** (profit-taking) consistently *hurt* (−0.7pp) — selling the
  crash before it finishes; see the full-sweep notes.
